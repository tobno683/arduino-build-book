What to buy

You do not need much, and the expensive-looking things are mostly not the things worth paying for. Here is what to buy first, what to buy when you get serious, where to buy it, and the specific traps in the listings.

Where to buy, honestly

SupplierPriceWaitWhat you are really buying
AliExpress1×2-5 weeks The cheapest possible version of everything. The parts usually work. The listing photo is often not the part you get, the datasheet is a JPEG of another datasheet, and "DHT22" sometimes arrives as a DHT11 in a DHT22 case. Buy commodity things here: jumper wires, resistors, breadboards, headers, enclosures, LEDs, generic modules you already know how to test.
Amazon2-3×1-2 days The same Chinese modules, marked up, in a box tomorrow. The quality lottery is identical - the seller is often the same factory - but returns actually work. Good for "I want to build this weekend".
Adafruit / SparkFun / Pimoroni3-5×Days A part that is genuinely what it says, a schematic, a library that is maintained, a tutorial, and a forum where someone answers. For your first use of any unfamiliar sensor this is worth every cent, because when it does not work you will know the fault is yours.
DigiKey / MouserVariesDays Real component distributors. Not module shops - you go here for the actual chip, the exact resistor tolerance, the connector with the right part number, and a real datasheet. Shipping often costs more than your order until you hit their free threshold.

A note on prices on this site

Every figure in the tables is a typical USD street price, because that is the only currency the whole hobby quotes in. Swedish prices include 25 % moms and land roughly two to three times higher. A project listed here at $25 is realistically 600-700 SEK from Electrokit, or close to the listed price if you are willing to wait three weeks for AliExpress.

The strategy that works

Buy the board cheap and the sensor good, at least the first time. An Uno clone is electrically identical to an official one for $20 less. A $1 BME280 that is secretly a BMP280 will cost you a whole evening.

Should you buy a starter kit?

The $35 "Arduino Super Starter Kit" boxes are genuinely good value if you treat them as a bag of passives rather than a curriculum. What you actually get:

  • Worth having: the breadboard, the jumper wires, a big resistor assortment, LEDs, buttons, a potentiometer, a servo, an ultrasonic sensor, an IR remote, a battery clip. Buying those separately costs about the same.
  • Usually disappointing: the DHT11 (buy a DHT22), the 1602 LCD without an I2C backpack (six wasted pins), the tiny 170-point breadboard, the "9V battery" that powers a servo for four minutes.
  • The booklet: a list of 15 sketches with no explanation of why anything works. Fine as a checklist, useless as a teacher.

Verdict: buy one if you own nothing, skip the branded "official" kit at three times the price, and do not expect it to contain the specific sensor for the project you actually want to build.

The listing traps, by part

PartWhat to check before you click buy
Arduino Uno cloneCH340 USB chip needs a driver installed; the FTDI-based ones can hit counterfeit-chip problems with some drivers. Either is fine - just know which you have.
OLED displayI2C (4 pins) or SPI (7 pins)? 0.96 inch is SSD1306, 1.3 inch is usually SH1106, and they need different libraries. Pin order varies between GND-VCC-SCL-SDA and VCC-GND-SCL-SDA.
BME280Under $2 it is a BMP280 - pressure and temperature but no humidity. Also comes in 4-pin (I2C only) and 6-pin (I2C or SPI) variants.
WS2812B strip5 V or 12 V (these projects assume 5 V). 30/60/144 LEDs per metre. IP30 bare, IP65 sleeved, IP67 filled. All four choices matter.
Relay moduleLook for the little optocoupler chips and a jumper marked JD-VCC. Without opto-isolation, a motor's back-EMF can reset or kill your board.
TP4056 chargerOnly buy the version with the protection IC - two extra chips near the USB socket. The plain one will over-discharge and destroy your cell.
Soil moisture sensorCapacitive only. The two-pronged resistive ones corrode away in weeks and read nonsense long before that.
Buzzer"Passive" plays whatever tone you generate; "active" plays one fixed beep and ignores your melody.
MOSFETMust be logic level (IRLZ44N, not IRF540) to switch fully from a 5 V pin. The wrong one gets hot and half-works.
nRF24L01Buy the plain one plus a 10 µF capacitor, or the version with the socket adapter. Without a capacitor on its supply it resets randomly.
ESP32-CAMGet the bundle with the MB programmer shield. Without it you need an FTDI adapter and a jumper on IO0 every time you upload.
Jumper wiresGet all three genders (M-M, M-F, F-F). The 10 cm ones are too short for anything real; 20 cm is the useful length.

What you can skip

  • A hot air rework station - only for surface-mount. Nothing on this site needs it.
  • An oscilloscope - genuinely useful eventually, useless before you know what you are looking for. A $25 multimeter answers 95 % of beginner questions.
  • A bench power supply - a phone charger and a USB meter cover most of it. Buy one when you are debugging something that keeps browning out.
  • A 3D printer - lovely, not required. Project boxes and cardboard work.
  • "Sensor kits" with 37 modules - about eight of them are useful and you will never identify the rest.
  • Expensive solder - any 0.8 mm rosin-core from a reputable brand is fine.
